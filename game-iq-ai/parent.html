How Jordan played

No soccer jargon. Here's what went well, what to work on, and how to help.

The short version

Strong game

Jordan had a genuinely good match in a 2-1 win and set up the first goal. The standout habit is looking around before getting the ball — most kids this age don't do it, and it makes Jordan look calm and smart on the field. The main thing to grow is confidence: when a riskier forward pass is on, Jordan usually picks the safe one. That's normal at this age and very coachable.

What the scores mean

  • Technical 76 — how clean the touches, passes and first touches are. Above average for the age group.
  • Tactical 71 — decisions and positioning. Reads the game well; could be braver.
  • Mechanics 68 — how the body moves: running, stopping, kicking. A clear area to improve, which is exciting because it's trainable.
Scores compare Jordan to other U13 competitive players — never to professionals.

Two things to celebrate

Praise these
  • Jordan checks over both shoulders before getting the ball — a real strength.
  • Fast over the first few steps and works hard to get back and defend.

One thing to encourage

Gently

When Jordan has the ball and a teammate is open further forward, encourage the braver pass — even when it doesn't come off. Confidence grows from being allowed to try. Please don't coach the mechanics from the sideline; that's what the drills below are for.

How to help at home

30 min, 3x a week
Wall passing

Just a ball and a wall. Builds the touch and the weaker left foot.

Praise the brave pass

After games, mention one forward pass Jordan tried — outcome doesn't matter.

Keep filming

One game a week is enough to see real progress month to month.
