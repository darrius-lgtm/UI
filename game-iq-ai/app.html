The science of the game

Built on soccer biomechanics

Six mechanics categories

  • Running — acceleration, deceleration, stride, balance, change of direction.
  • Kicking — support foot, hip-knee-ankle sequencing, contact and follow-through.
  • Receiving — body shape, first-touch angle, control under pressure.
  • Movement science — braking, re-acceleration, turning, posture in duels.
  • Ball physics — pass speed, trajectory and spin, where the footage allows.
  • Tactical mechanics — scanning, support angles, spacing, pressing triggers.
Every section carries a High / Medium / Low confidence label. We never claim what the camera can't see — and even a 30-second clip returns useful analysis.

Executive summary

Mechanics breakdown

Medium confidence

Running mechanics

7.4
  • Good acceleration mechanics over the first three steps — low body lean, strong knee drive.
  • Upright deceleration before change of direction. Center of mass stays high, so cuts lose half a yard.
  • Balanced stride pattern; re-acceleration after turns is above the age benchmark.

Kicking mechanics

6.3
  • Support foot too far from the ball on strike — reduces contact quality on driven passes.
  • Hip-knee-ankle sequencing is sound; good posture at strike on the right foot.
  • Follow-through cuts short on the weak foot, leaving left-footed passes under-hit.

Receiving mechanics

7.1
  • Open body shape when checking into space on 7 of 10 receptions.
  • Receiving on the back foot under pressure collapses to a safe touch — rarely opens out to play forward.
  • First-touch angle generally moves the ball into the next action, not under the feet.

Movement science

6.6
  • Poor braking posture before defensive cut — heel-first plant instead of a loaded, lower base.
  • Movement efficiency is high; little wasted motion between actions.
  • Turning quality is clean to the right, noticeably tighter than to the left.

Ball physics

Low confidence
  • Driven passes carry good pace; lofted balls tend to hang. Inferred from flight, not measured.
  • Limited spin/curve on switches of play. Single-angle footage caps confidence here.

Tactical mechanics

7.2
  • Scanning before receiving — 4.1 shoulder checks per 10 seconds in midfield.
  • Support angle after pass is often flat; rarely offers a forward bounce option.
  • Reads pressing triggers early in transition; body orientation before decisions is mostly open.

On-ball analysis

High
  • 47 touches, mostly central between halfway and the attacking third. 3 losses.
  • Receives side-on with an open body on 7 of 10 receptions — above age benchmark.
  • Under back-pressure, releases in under 1 second and rarely turns — 2 of 9 attempts to play forward off the half-turn.

Off-ball analysis

High

  • Good covering runs when the fullback pushed up; recovered position within 6 seconds on average.
  • Delayed recovery run after turnover on 4 occasions — ball-watching for a beat before sprinting back.

How Jordan played

No soccer jargon. Here's what went well, what to work on, and how to help.

The short version

Strong game

Jordan had a genuinely good match in a 2-1 win and set up the first goal. The standout habit is looking around before getting the ball — most kids this age don't do it, and it makes Jordan look calm and smart on the field. The main thing to grow is confidence: when a riskier forward pass is on, Jordan usually picks the safe one. That's normal at this age and very coachable.

What the scores mean

  • Technical 76 — how clean the touches, passes and first touches are. Above average for the age group.
  • Tactical 71 — decisions and positioning. Reads the game well; could be braver.
  • Mechanics 68 — how the body moves: running, stopping, kicking. A clear area to improve, which is exciting because it's trainable.
Scores compare Jordan to other U13 competitive players — never to professionals.

Two things to celebrate

Praise these
  • Jordan checks over both shoulders before getting the ball — a real strength.
  • Fast over the first few steps and works hard to get back and defend.

One thing to encourage

Gently

When Jordan has the ball and a teammate is open further forward, encourage the braver pass — even when it doesn't come off. Confidence grows from being allowed to try. Please don't coach the mechanics from the sideline; that's what the drills below are for.

How to help at home

30 min, 3x a week
Wall passing

Just a ball and a wall. Builds the touch and the weaker left foot.

Praise the brave pass

After games, mention one forward pass Jordan tried — outcome doesn't matter.

Keep filming

One game a week is enough to see real progress month to month.
